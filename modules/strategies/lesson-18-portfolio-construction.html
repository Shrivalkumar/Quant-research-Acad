Portfolio Construction for Stock Strategies

Turn scores into eligible, capped, cash-aware holdings while managing rebalance timing, turnover, concentration, and liquidity.

Intuition first

Portfolio construction decides which signal risks become real money

Universe selection defines what could have been owned. Eligibility applies point-in-time rules such as valid history and rolling traded value. Ranking orders eligible names. Selection chooses candidates. Weighting allocates capital. Rebalancing converts current holdings to targets. Each layer can create bias or cost even when the score is causal.

Equal weight is transparent but ignores different volatility. Risk weighting reduces high-volatility names but relies on noisy estimates. Frequent rebalancing follows changing ranks closely and pays more cost; infrequent rebalancing tolerates stale positions. Buffers—keep a holding until rank falls beyond the entry cutoff—can lower churn but add a parameter.

With a 15% per-name cap, seven positions at 15% would request 105%, impossible without leverage. Six at 15% use 90%, leaving 10% cash. Ten equal weights use 10% each. Fifteen equal weights use 6.67%. The harness also prevents adding to an existing position, so “rebalance weights” is not a generic continuous optimizer.

Questions beginners actually ask

Clear answers before we add mathematics

Why filter eligibility before ranking?

A ranking algorithm always tries to order the values it receives. If invalid, illiquid, or too-young stocks enter first, the rank no longer represents a tradeable historical choice.

Why does rebalancing create costs?

Every sale and purchase crosses real or modeled friction. Small rank changes can cause repeated replacements, so a stable-looking monthly score can still generate large annual turnover.

Follow the mechanism slowly

What happens first, second, third, and fourth

01

Form the eligible universe

At the decision close, remove names without sufficient history, price validity, or historical liquidity.

02

Score and select

Rank only eligible names, then apply selection and any retention buffer. Make tie handling deterministic.

03

Compare targets with holdings

Generate whole-position exits and genuinely new buys under the harness no-add rule. Reserve slots and cash.

04

Simulate the next day

Apply gaps, order priority, costs, and missing prices. The resulting portfolio—not the target list—determines exposure and P&L.

Deeper explanation

Rebalancing is a constrained transition, not a fresh portfolio

On a rebalance date you already own positions with gains and losses. Desired candidates may overlap with holdings, cash may be limited, and the harness forbids adding to an open position. You cannot simply pretend the old portfolio disappears and replace it with a clean target vector.

Construct exits first, then new entries. Predict cash after market sells and before stop sells. List buys deterministically because order sequence controls which triggers receive scarce slots or cash.

Turnover can come from rank instability rather than new information

Stocks near the cutoff may exchange ranks frequently due to tiny score changes. A top-ten portfolio that replaces rank 10 with rank 11 every rebalance pays cost without a meaningful change in evidence.

A retention buffer or less frequent schedule can reduce churn, but both add choices. Test whether savings survive nearby settings and whether slower changes leave the portfolio exposed to stale losers. Report measured turnover rather than assuming monthly means cheap.

Worked walkthrough

Cash-aware replacement

Equity at decision close = ₹1,000,000; eight holdings. Sell two at next open for ₹240,000 gross and 0.2% cost, receiving ₹239,520. Two new 10% buy orders target ₹100,000 each based on prior close equity. Because market sells execute first, their proceeds can fund buys. If they were stop sells, proceeds would arrive after the buy loop and could not fund same-day purchases.

Implementation lens

Eligibility then ranking

traded_value = close * volume
liq = traded_value.rolling(60, min_periods=40).median()
history = close.notna().rolling(126).sum()
eligible = (liq >= 1e8) & (history >= 126)
score_today = momentum.iloc[-1].where(eligible.iloc[-1])
candidates = score_today.nlargest(k).index.tolist()

Why this matters for my H TO H Partners assignment

Failure prevention

Common mistakes

  • Ranking first and filtering afterward
  • Requesting seven 15% positions
  • Ignoring existing holdings and no-add rule
  • Calling a monthly portfolio “low turnover” without measuring trades
Retrieval practice

Exercises

1. Seven positions at 15% request total exposure of…

  1. 95%
  2. 100%
  3. 105%
  4. 15%
Reveal explanation

Answer: C. 7×15%=105%, exceeding cash-only capital.

2. Why can order list order matter?

  1. Python is random
  2. Stop buys fill until slots/cash are exhausted in listed order
  3. Prices depend on alphabet
  4. Sells never execute
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. The harness processes buys sequentially.

3. A daily liquidity filter removes survivor bias?

  1. Completely
  2. No, missing failed names remain absent
  3. Only for Nifty
  4. Only after 2024
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. Filtering survivors cannot restore excluded histories.
