How Quantitative Researchers Think

Turn an intuition into a falsifiable economic hypothesis, define failure before testing, and resist random parameter search.

⏱ 75 min● Concept + calculation + practice⌁ H TO H assignment linked
Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Write a hypothesis with mechanism and prediction
  • Distinguish an anomaly from a durable edge
  • Pre-register an experiment and rejection rule
  • Discuss capacity, crowding, and signal decay

Intuition first

Research begins with what would make you wrong

An alpha hypothesis links a mechanism to an observable prediction. “Moving averages work” is not enough. A stronger statement is: gradual information diffusion and behavioral anchoring may create directional persistence at multi-week horizons; therefore a causal trend state should have positive subsequent expectancy, particularly after broad moves, but fail in rapid mean-reverting markets.

Economic rationale does not prove profitability. Behavioral explanations include underreaction, herding, and slow institutional repositioning. Risk-based explanations say returns compensate for unpleasant exposure, such as momentum crashes. A pattern can decay when discovered, become crowded, or disappear after costs and capacity limits.

Before running, record data, feature family, parameter range, benchmark, primary metric, secondary risks, and rejection conditions. After seeing results, diagnostics may motivate a new hypothesis—but label it as a new experiment. Otherwise every explanation becomes a story fitted to the curve.

Questions beginners actually ask

Clear answers before we add mathematics

What makes an idea a research hypothesis?

It must state why an effect could exist, what observable result should follow, and what evidence would make you reject or revise it. “This chart looks good” is an observation, not a hypothesis.

Why decide failure conditions before testing?

After seeing results, people naturally invent explanations that protect a preferred idea. Predeclared failure conditions make the experiment capable of changing your mind.

Follow the mechanism slowly

What happens first, second, third, and fourth

01

Write the mechanism

Explain whose behavior or risk could create the pattern. A mechanism must make sense before looking at the preferred parameter.

02

Make a directional prediction

State what should improve, where it should work, and where it should fail. Include costs and a baseline.

03

Run the smallest discriminating test

Change as few moving parts as possible. A minimal experiment teaches more than a complex combination whose contribution cannot be separated.

04

Update belief honestly

Keep, revise, or reject using predeclared criteria. A new diagnostic insight becomes a new experiment, not a retroactive explanation.

Deeper explanation

Economic rationale limits the search space

If the hypothesis is gradual information diffusion, relevant horizons should reflect how slowly information is believed to spread. Testing every lookback from 2 to 500 bars because computation is cheap abandons the mechanism and increases selection bias.

A rationale also predicts failures. A trend mechanism should struggle during rapid reversals; a liquidity premium should weaken when trading costs rise; a cross-sectional effect may concentrate in certain types of stocks. When observed behavior contradicts these predictions, update the hypothesis instead of inventing a protective story.

Research is a sequence of belief updates

Before a run, write what you expect. Afterward, separate facts from interpretation: “turnover doubled” is a fact; “the signal is too noisy” is an interpretation. Decide what additional measurement would distinguish competing explanations.

This process produces a credible failed-ideas document naturally. A failed idea is not merely one with low Sharpe—it can be rejected because behavior contradicted the mechanism, depended on one year, or became uneconomic after realistic friction.

Worked walkthrough

Pre-registering a minimal experiment

Question: does a slow trend state improve returns after costs relative to always-long? Prediction: lower drawdown and lower exposure, with underperformance during sharp reversals. Fixed choices: one feature family, three adjacent lookbacks, next-open fill, public harness costs. Reject if: sign reverses across adjacent settings, cost doubling destroys all excess return, or one year contributes nearly everything. This structure does not reveal a final Task 1 strategy; it defines honest learning.

Assignment connection

Why this matters for my H TO H Partners assignment

The assignment requires failed ideas, parameter inventories, sensitivity, and hidden validation precisely to examine research process. Record actual results; do not reverse-engineer a polished narrative after finding a winner.

Failure prevention

Common mistakes

  • Writing the mechanism after seeing the curve
  • Treating a paper’s average finding as guaranteed locally
  • Adding filters until drawdown disappears
  • Calling every failed test a “regime issue” without prediction
Retrieval practice

Exercises

1. Which is most falsifiable?

  1. Momentum works
  2. A predeclared trend state should improve subsequent net expectancy and fail in whipsaws
  3. Markets are emotional
  4. The best parameter is 63
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. It specifies mechanism, outcome, costs, and failure environment.

2. Crowding can make an edge…

  1. Riskless
  2. More vulnerable to synchronized unwind
  3. Independent of capacity
  4. Free of costs
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. Similar positions can exit together.

3. A rejected causal experiment is…

  1. Always wasted
  2. Useful if it narrows the hypothesis space
  3. A reason to fabricate numbers
  4. Proof markets are efficient
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. Research value includes learning what fails and why.
