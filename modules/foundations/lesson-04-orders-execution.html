Understanding Orders and Execution

Reason about next-open fills, stops, overnight gaps, slippage, costs, and why a plausible signal can become an implausible backtest.

⏱ 55 min● Concept + calculation + practice⌁ H TO H assignment linked
Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Predict market, limit, and stop fills across a price gap
  • Distinguish a stop order from a guaranteed stop-loss price
  • Trace the Task 2 next-day order priority
  • Explain why execution lag is a causal requirement, not a nuisance

Intuition first

The signal is an idea; the fill is the price you can actually obtain

A strategy sees the close only after the bar is complete. If it decides using that close, the earliest clean modeled market fill is the next open. Crediting the just-finished bar’s return is time travel. Overnight news can cause the next open to gap far from the close, and that gap belongs to whoever held the position overnight—not to an order invented after yesterday’s close.

A buy stop says “activate if price trades above a trigger.” It is commonly used to enter a breakout. In the Task 2 harness, the next day must have high > trigger; the fill is the worse of the open or trigger, max(open, trigger). Thus a ₹100 buy stop on a ₹106 gap opens at ₹106, not ₹100. A sell stop fills at min(open, trigger). These rules prevent backtests from assuming execution at a price the market jumped over.

Slippage is the difference between a reference price and achieved execution; transaction cost includes explicit and implicit frictions. The harness bundles Task 2 friction into 0.2% per side. It is a model, not a universal fee schedule. Robust conclusions should survive a meaningful increase in that assumption.

Questions beginners actually ask

Clear answers before we add mathematics

Why can I not use today’s close as both my signal and my fill?

The final close is known only after the trading interval finishes. A rule that needs that close cannot have traded earlier at the same price unless a real executable closing-auction mechanism was modeled. The assignment avoids that ambiguity by using the next open.

Is a stop-loss a guaranteed maximum loss?

No. A stop is an instruction that becomes active after a trigger. If price gaps beyond the trigger, the realistic fill can be much worse. Stops control the response to observed prices; they do not control what prices the market will offer.

Follow the mechanism slowly

What happens first, second, third, and fourth

01

Observe only completed information

A close-based rule must wait until the close exists. Write down the exact timestamp at which every input becomes available.

02

Create the order after the decision

Choose market, limit, or stop behavior and specify how long the instruction remains valid. In Task 2 it expires after the next trading day.

03

Apply the next eligible market path

The next open may gap. Stop triggers use the next high/low and a gap-aware fill; missing prices cause the order to lapse.

04

Update cash and holdings in engine order

Market sells can release cash before buys; stop sells occur later. The same order list can produce different holdings if processing order is changed.

Deeper explanation

Market, limit, and stop orders solve different problems

A market order says execution matters more than exact price. A limit order says price protection matters more than certainty of execution. A stop order says no order should be active until a trigger is crossed. A stop-market entry can therefore combine conditional participation with uncertain fill price. These are not “better” and “worse” versions of one order; they express different priorities.

In daily OHLC data, the engine cannot know the full sequence of trades. The Task 2 harness uses a consistent rule: a buy stop requires the next high to exceed the trigger and fills at the worse of open or trigger. That rule is conservative about overnight gaps. It still cannot model queue position, intraday spread, partial fills, or a high occurring after a conflicting low.

Why execution errors are often larger than indicator errors

A moving average may be mathematically correct while the backtest applies its target one row too early. That single timing mistake can award the strategy the same return that created the signal. The resulting curve can look smooth and plausible because the error happens consistently.

Debug execution using event statements: “At close t I know these values. I create this order. At open t+1 it fills at this price. From that instant onward this holding earns returns.” Write one line per event and compare it with the engine arrays. This discipline is more reliable than staring at a full equity curve.

Worked walkthrough

Gap-through stop

Decision close: ₹98. Buy-stop trigger: ₹100. Next day open/high/low: ₹106/₹110/₹104. Since high > 100, the order triggers; fill = max(₹106, ₹100) = ₹106. With ₹15,000 allocated and 0.2% buy cost, shares = 15,000×0.998/106 = 141.226. A backtest filling at ₹100 would invent a 6% gain before the market was accessible.

Implementation lens

Testing fill logic

def buy_stop_fill(open_, high_, trigger):
    if high_ > trigger:
        return max(open_, trigger)
    return None

assert buy_stop_fill(106, 110, 100) == 106

Educational pattern—adapt only after you can explain every line.

Assignment connection

Why this matters for my H TO H Partners assignment

Task 1 always turns the close-i target into the open-i+1 position. Task 2 orders live for exactly one next trading day. The interview can expose timing confusion quickly, so draw the event timeline before discussing any indicator.

Failure prevention

Common mistakes

  • Filling a stop at its trigger after an overnight gap
  • Keeping unfilled Task 2 stops alive indefinitely
  • Using close t to earn the return during t
  • Assuming stop-loss means maximum loss is known
Retrieval practice

Exercises

1. A buy stop trigger is ₹100; next open ₹103 and high ₹105. Harness fill?

  1. ₹100
  2. ₹103
  3. ₹105
  4. No fill
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. It triggers and fills at max(open, trigger) = ₹103.

2. Why delay a close-based signal to the next open?

  1. To lower returns
  2. Because the full close was unavailable before that bar ended
  3. Because markets forbid close trades
  4. Only to add costs
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. The lag enforces causal information timing.

3. A stop sell executes after buys in Task 2. Same-day proceeds can fund those buys?

  1. Yes
  2. No
  3. Only for Nifty stocks
  4. Only below 15%
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. The buy loop is already complete.
