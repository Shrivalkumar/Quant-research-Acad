Statistics for Traders

Use distributions, uncertainty, conditional reasoning, and sampling variation without mistaking a lucky history for a law.

⏱ 75 min● Concept + calculation + practice⌁ H TO H assignment linked
Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Calculate mean, variance, and standard deviation
  • Explain skew, heavy tails, and outliers
  • Distinguish correlation from causation
  • Interpret a standard error as uncertainty, not a verdict

Intuition first

A backtest is one noisy sample from a process that may be changing

The sample mean summarizes average return, but two strategies with the same mean can have radically different dispersion and tail behavior. Variance averages squared deviations; standard deviation returns to the original unit. Financial returns are often skewed and heavy-tailed, so a normal curve can underestimate extreme events.

Conditional probability asks how an outcome changes given information—for example, the chance of a positive next month given positive trailing momentum. A historical difference can arise from an effect, noise, selection, or a third variable. Correlation quantifies co-movement, not causal mechanism.

The standard error of an estimated mean shrinks roughly with the square root of independent observations. Market returns are not perfectly independent: autocorrelation, volatility clustering, and overlapping lookbacks reduce effective information. A t-statistic is not a shield against data snooping. If you try hundreds of strategies and report the luckiest, its apparent significance is biased upward.

Questions beginners actually ask

Clear answers before we add mathematics

Does a positive average prove a strategy works?

No. The average may be driven by one extreme event or sampling luck, and the uncertainty can be large. You must inspect dispersion, tails, sample length, dependence, costs, and how many alternatives were tried.

What does statistical significance mean in plain language?

Under a chosen mathematical model, it asks how surprising the observed result would be if there were no effect. It does not prove the model assumptions, economic mechanism, future persistence, or freedom from data-mining.

Follow the mechanism slowly

What happens first, second, third, and fourth

01

Describe the sample

Start with count, mean, median, dispersion, extremes, skew, and missingness. This reveals whether one observation dominates.

02

State the uncertainty model

A standard error or test depends on assumptions about independence and distribution. Market observations often violate the simplest assumptions.

03

Account for search

If many signals were tried, the best result is selected from noise as well as any true effect. Record the number and structure of attempts.

04

Interpret instead of declaring

Use statistical evidence together with mechanism, robustness, and economic size. “Significant” is not synonymous with profitable, causal, or permanent.

Deeper explanation

Why financial averages are unstable

Daily returns contain much more noise than signal. A few crisis or rebound days can materially change the mean, standard deviation, and Sharpe. Extending the sample adds observations but may also add a different regime, so uncertainty is both statistical and economic.

Outliers deserve investigation, not automatic deletion. Ask whether the observation is a genuine market move, execution artifact, corporate action, or data error. If genuine, it may be exactly the risk the strategy must survive. If erroneous, correction should follow a general rule and documented source evidence.

Multiple testing changes how impressive the winner is

If 100 independent useless strategies are tested, some will look good by chance. Real research variants are correlated rather than independent, but selection still inflates the best result. Reporting only the winner hides this search.

Control the problem by beginning with mechanisms, limiting parameter ranges, preserving a final test, showing neighboring results, and logging every experiment. Statistical corrections can help, but they cannot rescue an unlimited, undocumented search on one history.

Worked walkthrough

Mean and sample standard deviation

Returns are 1%, −1%, 2%, and 0%. Mean = 0.5%. Deviations are 0.5, −1.5, 1.5, −0.5 percentage points. Squared deviations sum to 5.0. Sample variance = 5/(4−1)=1.6667 squared points; sample SD = 1.291 percentage points. With only four observations, this estimate is extremely uncertain.

Implementation lens

Describe a return sample

r = pd.Series([0.01, -0.01, 0.02, 0.00])
mean = r.mean()
sd = r.std(ddof=1)
sem_naive = sd / np.sqrt(r.count())
# 'naive' because dependence may reduce effective sample size

Educational pattern—adapt only after you can explain every line.

Assignment connection

Why this matters for my H TO H Partners assignment

Sharpe is a ratio of a noisy mean to noisy volatility. A high value from 2.3 years and many tried variants is weaker evidence than the same value from a predeclared test across diverse regimes. Hidden data is intended to expose this gap.

Failure prevention

Common mistakes

  • Treating median as expected compounded growth
  • Assuming zero correlation proves independence
  • Using every overlapping window as independent evidence
  • Interpreting p<0.05 after many trials as certain truth
Retrieval practice

Exercises

1. Two strategies have the same mean. Can their risk differ?

  1. No
  2. Yes, through dispersion and tails
  3. Only if currencies differ
  4. Only after costs
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. Mean does not determine variance, skew, or drawdown.

2. Correlation between a signal and returns proves…

  1. Causation
  2. Only measured co-movement in that sample
  3. Profit after costs
  4. Persistence
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. Mechanism and validation are still needed.

3. Trying 500 variants and reporting one raises…

  1. Settlement risk only
  2. Multiple-testing / selection bias
  3. Dividend yield
  4. Margin
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. The selected winner benefited from the search itself.
