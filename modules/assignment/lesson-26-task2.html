How to Approach Task 2 Independently

Research a long-only stock momentum portfolio with point-in-time eligibility, realistic orders, caps, costs, attribution, and bias-aware conclusions.

⏱ 100 min● Concept + calculation + practice⌁ H TO H assignment linked
Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Build eligibility and ranking causally
  • Map candidates to constrained orders
  • Diagnose concentration, turnover, anomalies, and top trades
  • Explain why naive six-month top-10 can fail hidden data

Intuition first

A stock score is only the first sentence of a portfolio story

Begin with the universe limitation: all 555 names were selected for October 2026 liquidity. Build daily historical eligibility from listing age, valid prices, and rolling traded value, but do not claim it removes survivor bias. Decide whether momentum is absolute, relative, risk-adjusted, or conditional, and state what should break it.

At each decision close, rank only eligible stocks. Compare proposed candidates with current holdings. Generate whole-position sells and capped new buys. Predict next-day behavior when prices gap, symbols lack prices, more stop orders trigger than slots, or stop-sell proceeds arrive after buys.

Analyze name/sector concentration, holding periods, turnover, entry gaps, closed winners/losers, open positions, and the percentage of net profit contributed by the top ten closed profitable trades under a clearly stated denominator. Remove the best calendar year from daily equity consistently. Investigate the flagged SPLPETRO event with a general anomaly policy rather than a symbol/date exception.

The naive six-month top-10 warning indicates historical rank persistence was regime-dependent and/or concentrated. Plausible causes include winner crowding, reversals, beta, sector concentration, and costs. Test mechanisms—not a secret workaround tailored to the hidden calendar.

Questions beginners actually ask

Clear answers before we add mathematics

Can I remove the SPLPETRO crash because the README says it may be an anomaly?

You need a general, predeclared treatment for suspicious corporate-action jumps and must report it. A symbol-and-date exception chosen after seeing its effect is hard-coding and undermines hidden-data robustness.

What does top-ten trade contribution tell me?

It reveals whether a small number of lucky winners generated most net profit. Very high concentration does not automatically invalidate a strategy, but it makes the evidence more fragile and must be explained.

Follow the mechanism slowly

What happens first, second, third, and fourth

01

Acknowledge the universe limitation

The 555 names were selected by October 2026 liquidity. Historical filters improve tradability but cannot recreate excluded failures.

02

Create one dated ranking snapshot

At each close, compute eligibility and scores using only historical rows. Inspect young listings and extreme returns.

03

Build feasible orders

Respect current holdings, no additions, 15 slots, 15% cap, cash, and next-day-only validity.

04

Attribute the result

Measure costs, turnover, holding periods, concentration, best-year dependence, top trades, anomalies, and what remains after robust stresses.

Deeper explanation

Point-in-time ranking is a daily reconstruction problem

For each date, imagine deleting every later row. Which symbols have enough history? Which meet the trailing liquidity rule? What are their scores? Which are already held? This mental reconstruction is the standard for causality.

Do not compute a final full-sample statistic and slice it backward unless you can prove every value was formed only from trailing data. Check several dates manually, including early evaluation, a young listing, and the SPLPETRO event.

What a convincing Task 2 result would look like

Returns should survive realistic costs and not be explained entirely by market beta, one sector, the best year, or ten exceptional trades. Eligibility should be causal, order construction feasible, and portfolio constraints respected under gaps and missing prices.

Because the universe is survivor-selected, even a strong result must be framed as conditional. The hidden test provides additional evidence, but no public-data technique can prove how excluded delisted names would have changed history.

Worked walkthrough

Top-trade contribution

Suppose closed profitable trades contribute ₹40k, ₹25k, ₹15k… and all closed trade P&L nets to ₹100k after losing trades. If top ten winners total ₹120k, contribution is 120%. That is possible because remaining trades net −₹20k. State whether denominator is total closed-trade net profit or portfolio net profit; open holdings and cash/cost accounting can make them differ.

Implementation lens

Causal snapshot scaffold

def research_snapshot(hist):
    c = hist['close'].iloc[-300:]
    v = hist['volume'].iloc[-300:]
    liq = (c*v).rolling(60, min_periods=40).median().iloc[-1]
    # define and justify score and minimum history yourself
    return liq

Failure prevention

Common mistakes

  • Using a date exception for SPLPETRO
  • Calling survivors a representative historical universe
  • Ranking stocks with insufficient history
  • Computing top-trade contribution without defining net-profit denominator
Retrieval practice

Exercises

1. Historical liquidity eligibility should use…

  1. Future October 2026 volume
  2. Trailing data available by decision close
  3. Full-sample median
  4. Next-day volume
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. Eligibility must be point-in-time.

2. Why can top-ten contribution exceed 100%?

  1. Impossible
  2. Other trades can net lose money
  3. Only leverage
  4. Because CAGR is annualized
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. Winners can exceed total net when losers offset them.

3. The six-month warning should cause you to…

  1. Invert six-month momentum automatically
  2. Research mechanisms and robustness without hidden-date tailoring
  3. Skip momentum entirely
  4. Hard-code 2024
Reveal explanation

Answer: B. The lesson is fragility, not a secret parameter hint.
