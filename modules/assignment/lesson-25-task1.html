Intuition first

The goal is not to find a curve; it is to understand why a position should survive the next open

Start by reproducing template execution and drawing the close→open timeline. Audit bars and expiries. State a minimal directional hypothesis and its expected failure regime. Choose one feature family and a small parameter neighborhood. Express target states, then verify them on labeled synthetic bars before running the full sample.

Interpret the first run as a diagnostic. Separate cash baseline from directional value. Inspect exposure, turnover, trade clusters, yearly return, drawdown path, carry/roll cost, and whether one crisis supplies most return. A strategy near the Sharpe threshold can still be fragile if neighboring settings, delayed fills, or doubled costs collapse.

Then predeclare a 3×3 grid, one- and two-bar delay, and cost stress. Keep the economic logic unchanged. Reject or revise based on evidence, not on whether another filter can rescue the public period.

Questions beginners actually ask

Clear answers before we add mathematics

What should my first Task 1 experiment be?

A minimal, interpretable hypothesis with one feature family and a small predeclared range—not a complex strategy. First prove the timing and accounting on synthetic bars, then learn from the baseline.

How do I know whether return is more than the cash baseline?

Compare the strategy’s daily equity return with the same 6% annual cash convention used by the harness. Exposure, turnover, and excess Sharpe help separate directional value from interest earned by capital.

02

03

Diagnose the baseline

Measure excess over cash, exposure, turnover, years, drawdown, carry, and roll dependence.

04

Earn the right to add complexity

Only a specific diagnosed failure should motivate one new component. Then use ablation and repeat the fixed robustness protocol.

Deeper explanation

Separate the market-timing question from the indicator

The research question is whether observable Nifty history contains directional information that survives execution and cost. A moving average, breakout, or momentum calculation is merely one measurement. Do not become loyal to an indicator before testing the underlying claim.

First examine feature buckets or simple states against subsequent returns. Then map evidence to long, flat, or short and measure what the mapping adds or destroys. This separates predictive failure from portfolio-design failure.

What a convincing Task 1 result would look like

A convincing result is not necessarily the highest CAGR. It has causal timing, material excess performance over cash, acceptable drawdown, manageable turnover, and behavior that matches the economic story. Neighboring settings remain reasonable, costs and delays degrade gradually, and returns are not solely one crisis trade.

The report should also explain failure: choppy reversals, carry on long exposure, violent rebounds against shorts, or signal decay. Naming these risks demonstrates understanding rather than weakness.

Worked walkthrough

Questions before any full run

Which price movement can this signal legitimately earn? Why should evidence persist after the next open? Would the idea prefer long, flat, or short in a fall? What happens in a V-shaped reversal? What proportion of return is merely 6% cash? How many one-way position changes occur per year? Write expected answers before viewing the curve.

Common mistakes
