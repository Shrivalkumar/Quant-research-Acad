Futures Carry, Basis, Expiry, and Rolling

Intuition first

A futures price embeds the economics of waiting

Basis is futures price minus spot. In a simplified equity-index model, holding the cash basket requires financing but earns dividends. A theoretical future therefore relates to spot roughly through financing minus dividend yield over time. Positive basis is often called contango; negative basis backwardation. Real basis also reflects demand, funding, taxes, and implementation constraints.

A contract expires. To keep exposure, a trader closes the near contract and opens a later one—a roll. Even if direction stays long, two contract trades occur. The pack does not contain futures quotes, so the harness models spot P&L plus a fixed annual carry of 6%−1.3%=4.7% charged to an overnight long and credited to a short. It also charges two sides of Task 1 cost on the night after a supplied expiry date.

Cash return is distinct: every Task 1 day adds 6%/252 to capital. A flat strategy therefore has positive nominal CAGR near the cash assumption. Evaluate strategy Sharpe in excess of this same 6% baseline, as the harness does.

Questions beginners actually ask

Clear answers before we add mathematics

Why can a futures price differ from the spot index?

A future settles later. Financing the cash basket costs money while the basket earns dividends, so time, interest, and expected income affect the fair relationship. Real demand, funding constraints, and trading pressure can also move the observed basis.

What exactly is rolled?

The expiring contract is closed and a later-expiry contract is opened. Directional exposure may remain long throughout, but two contract trades still occur. The assignment represents their friction with a fixed two-side roll charge.

Follow the mechanism slowly

What happens first, second, third, and fourth

01

Compare spot and future

Spot is the present index level. A future delivers dated exposure, so financing and expected dividends affect the theoretical difference.

02

Carry the position overnight

The assignment charges a long and credits a short using the fixed financing-minus-dividend assumption. This occurs only when exposure crosses a night.

03

Reach contract expiry

A real near-month contract cannot remain open forever. Maintaining direction requires moving into a later contract.

04

Pay the modeled roll

The harness charges two sides after a supplied expiry even when target direction does not change. Cash interest remains a separate daily credit.

Deeper explanation

The no-arbitrage intuition behind equity-index futures

Imagine two ways to obtain index exposure until expiry: buy the cash basket today using borrowed money, or enter a futures contract. The cash basket requires financing but receives constituent dividends. If the future became implausibly expensive relative to this replication after costs, arbitrageurs could buy the cheaper route and sell the expensive route. This pressure connects futures and spot.

A simplified fair-value relationship is therefore spot grown by financing minus dividend yield over the remaining time. “Simplified” matters: taxes, funding rates, actual dividend expectations, basket execution, and market pressure produce deviations. The assignment fixes financing and dividend assumptions rather than estimating a live basis series.

Read the Task 1 overnight equation in words

Across the night, the previously held position earns the gap from prior close to today’s open. A long pays the modeled futures carry; a short receives it. If the prior date is an expiry, either signed position pays the two-side roll cost. Separately, the account earns daily cash interest because the model treats margin capital as invested in a liquid fund.

These flows answer different questions. Gap return is market movement. Carry represents spot-versus-future economics. Roll cost represents replacing contracts. Cash return is the opportunity return on collateral. Combining them into one vague “overnight return” makes it harder to explain why long and short results differ.

Worked walkthrough

Carry and roll in rupees

On ₹1,000,000 capital, long overnight carry = ₹1,000,000×0.047/252 = ₹186.51 charged. Daily cash = ₹238.10 credited. Net of these two before price movement is +₹51.59. If the prior day is expiry, roll cost = ₹1,000,000×0.0005 = ₹500, making the modeled overnight net −₹448.41 before the gap. A short receives ₹186.51 carry and the same cash, but still pays ₹500 roll.

Implementation lens

Replicating the daily constants

cash_daily = 0.06 / 252
carry_daily = (0.06 - 0.013) / 252
long_overnight = cash_daily - carry_daily
roll = 2 * 0.00025 * abs(position)

Educational pattern—adapt only after you can explain every line.

Failure prevention

Common mistakes

  • Subtracting carry from shorts instead of adding it
  • Forgetting cash accrues even when invested
  • Charging roll only when the signal changes
  • Teaching the fixed 4.7% assumption as a live market fact
Retrieval practice

Reveal explanation

Answer: B. The roll models closing/opening contracts even while direction stays unchanged.
