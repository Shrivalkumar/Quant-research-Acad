Live Walkthrough Practice

Twenty realistic questions plus five standalone coding changes. Speak your reasoning before revealing the model explanation.

1. Explain your strategy in two minutes.

Ideal conceptual explanation
Lead with economic mechanism, information set, signal-to-position mapping, fill timing, risks, evidence, and one honest limitation.

2. Why should this edge persist?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Discuss behavioral or risk channel, frictions, capacity, crowding, and evidence that the effect is not one regime.

3. Show me where look-ahead is prevented.

Ideal conceptual explanation
Point to trailing windows, shifts, close decision, next-open fill, and a truncation or toy test.

4. Why these parameter ranges?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Tie them to horizon and turnover, disclose every tried value, and show neighboring sensitivity.

5. What does a Sharpe of 1.2 mean here?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Daily excess mean per unit daily volatility annualized by √252 under harness risk-free convention; noisy and incomplete.

6. Why not optimize for maximum CAGR?

Ideal conceptual explanation
It encourages concentration and overfit; use risk, robustness, costs, and mechanism.

7. What happens if costs double?

Ideal conceptual explanation
State measured effect and which turnover component drives it; do not guess if unmeasured.

8. Why does one-bar delay matter?

Ideal conceptual explanation
It measures signal decay and protects against execution assumptions.

9. What causes the worst drawdown?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Identify observed trade/regime mechanics while avoiding hindsight certainty.

10. How did you handle survivor bias?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Acknowledge direction and inability to reconstruct missing names; describe historical eligibility and conservative interpretation.

11. Why can win rate be low but strategy profitable?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Payoff asymmetry: average winners can exceed losers after costs.

12. Explain Task 2 cash flow on rebalance day.

Ideal conceptual explanation
Market sells, sequential buys, then stop sells; stop-sale cash is unavailable to earlier buys.

13. What does state contain?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Only persistent causal cache or scheduling information; no external/future data.

14. How would you add a new filter?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Write a new hypothesis, predict effect/failure, add one component, micro-test, run ablation and robustness.

15. How would you diagnose poor hidden results?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Do not retune on hidden; compare predicted failure modes, regimes, cost/delay sensitivity, concentration, and data assumptions.

16. Why is max drawdown not a future bound?

Ideal conceptual explanation
It is one realized historical path; unseen shocks and longer samples can be worse.

17. What is your benchmark?

Ideal conceptual explanation
State exact series, dividend treatment, period, and why risk differences limit conclusions.

18. Show an off-by-one bug.

Ideal conceptual explanation
Use a labeled tiny Series to compare numerator, shifted denominator, signal date, and fill date.

19. Which experiment did you reject?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Give pre-run rationale, exact parameters, measured numbers, failure diagnosis, and decision without embarrassment.

20. What would you improve with more time?

Ideal conceptual explanation
Choose one high-information experiment, not a list of complexity; explain why it reduces uncertainty.

Five coding challenges

  1. Challenge 1. Add a configurable one-bar extra delay without changing feature values. State expected output before coding; use synthetic data; explain complexity and edge cases.
  2. Challenge 2. Replace a calendar-month rebalance test with a fixed every-N-trading-days rule. State expected output before coding; use synthetic data; explain complexity and edge cases.
  3. Challenge 3. Write a toy assertion proving a close-t signal cannot earn the open→close-t return. State expected output before coding; use synthetic data; explain complexity and edge cases.
  4. Challenge 4. Add a historical eligibility mask requiring 60 valid closes and explain each NaN path. State expected output before coding; use synthetic data; explain complexity and edge cases.
  5. Challenge 5. Diagnose and fix an off-by-one momentum lookback on a five-row labeled Series. State expected output before coding; use synthetic data; explain complexity and edge cases.
