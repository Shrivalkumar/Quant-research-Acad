Task 2: Researching Stock Momentum Responsibly

A point-in-time process for eligibility, ranking, portfolio construction, costs, and bias-aware interpretation.

The research sequence

01

Audit the universe

The list was chosen for October 2026 liquidity; note missing failures and do not call it point-in-time representative.

02

Build eligibility before ranking

Use only historical listing age, valid prices, and lagged rolling liquidity. An ineligible stock must not receive a rank.

03

Specify the momentum claim

Decide whether the evidence is absolute, cross-sectional, volatility-adjusted, or market-regime conditional—and what should falsify it.

04

Create a causal snapshot

Every date uses only rows available through that close. Treat young listings and the SPLPETRO-like anomaly explicitly.

05

Map ranks to orders

Model sells, buys, caps, cash, next-open gaps, stop semantics, and order priority. Orders live for one day only.

06

Diagnose the portfolio

Inspect sector/name concentration, turnover, holding periods, entry gaps, winners, losers, and top-10 trade contribution.

07

Challenge naive 6-month top-10

The warning indicates a known train/hidden regime mismatch. Research why—crowding, concentration, reversal, market beta—not how to fit hidden dates.

08

Validate the family

Use best-year removal, 2× costs, sensitivity grids, and alternative historical subperiods without repeatedly selecting on the same holdout.

What you need to build → what you must understand

Build: a small, deterministic strategy interface that the harness can call. Understand: every timestamp, transformation, order, cost, accounting step, and metric. Questions: write expected behavior before each experiment. Common bugs: rank before filtering, shift the wrong axis, leak the full sample, mistake target for actual holdings, or ignore the next-open gap. Verify: micro-cases first, full history second, stress tests third.

Boundary

This guide intentionally stops before a final feature recipe, tuned parameter set, or strategy implementation. Your submission must be the result of your own hypotheses and measured experiments.
