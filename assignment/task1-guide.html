Task 1 laboratory

Task 1: Build the Research, Not the Answer

A staged method for investigating Nifty timing ideas without receiving a submission-ready strategy.

The research sequence

01

Audit the clock

Confirm bar-start labels, variable session length, 200-bar flat warm-up, close decisions, and next-open fills.

02

Write the hypothesis first

State why slow price adjustment, behavioral herding, or risk persistence might create directionality—and when choppy reversals should break it.

03

Choose one signal family

Compare a trend state, breakout, or time-series momentum idea conceptually. Do not combine filters until the baseline failure is understood.

04

Separate score from position

Turn continuous evidence into −1/0/+1 with explicit transition rules; measure how thresholds change exposure and turnover.

05

Run micro-tests

Use synthetic bars to test gaps, flips, overnight carry, expiry roll, and warm-up before trusting full-sample metrics.

06

Diagnose the path

Inspect year-wise return, exposure, trade clusters, time in drawdown, cost drag, and whether one crisis dominates.

07

Stress the implementation

One-bar delay, two-bar delay, 2× cost, and a predeclared 3×3 parameter grid.

08

Decide honestly

Keep only if neighboring parameters tell the same story and the economic explanation survives observed failures.

What you need to build → what you must understand

Build: a small, deterministic strategy interface that the harness can call. Understand: every timestamp, transformation, order, cost, accounting step, and metric. Questions: write expected behavior before each experiment. Common bugs: rank before filtering, shift the wrong axis, leak the full sample, mistake target for actual holdings, or ignore the next-open gap. Verify: micro-cases first, full history second, stress tests third.

Research questions to answer in your own notebook

Boundary

This guide intentionally stops before a final feature recipe, tuned parameter set, or strategy implementation. Your submission must be the result of your own hypotheses and measured experiments.
